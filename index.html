About

Luigi Glielmo earned his laurea in Electronic Engineering and his doctorate in Automatic Control at the University of Naples Federico II. His career has spanned Federico II, the University of Palermo, and more than two decades at the University of Sannio — where he founded GRACE, the Group for Research on Automatic Control Engineering — before returning to Federico II in 2022.

His research moves across singular perturbation methods, predictive and learning-based control, automotive systems, smart grids and energy communities, hydrogen storage, Boolean control networks, and deep brain stimulation. He has co-authored more than 220 papers, edited two books for Springer, and holds two patents. A Senior Member of IEEE and member of SIAM, he is Senior Editor of IEEE Transactions on Control Systems Technology and Vice-Chair of the Conference Board of IFAC.

Research

Themes

Predictive & learning-based control

Model predictive and Learning MPC for tasks that repeat: building safe sets and value functions from data so a controller improves itself iteration after iteration, with recursive feasibility and stability intact. Current threads include reduced-order and distributed Learning MPC.

Smart grids & energy communities

Optimization-based operation of microgrids and renewable energy communities — from stochastic MPC to experimental deployments — aimed at efficient, verifiable dispatch under uncertainty.

Hydrogen & renewable energy systems

Control architectures pairing wind generation with hydrogen storage and electrolysers, coordinating production and smooth grid injection through hierarchical predictive control.

Automotive & vehicle control

Powertrain and vehicle-dynamics control: catalytic-converter warm-up, battery state estimation, automated gearshift, and energy management for hybrid and electric vehicles.

Boolean control networks

Feedback stabilization, output tracking, and reinforcement-learning-based control of probabilistic Boolean networks — discrete models of gene-regulatory and logical systems.

Biomedical systems

Modeling and closed-loop control in the life sciences, notably deep brain stimulation for Parkinson's disease.

Teaching

Courses

At Federico II his teaching runs from System Theory through Advanced Control Methods and Applications to Learning for Dynamics and Control. He has also delivered graduate short courses internationally, including From convex optimization to reinforcement learning at Zhejiang Normal University (2024) and Université Grenoble Alpes (2024), and Control of energy systems at Johannes Kepler Universität Linz (2016–2021).

An intensive Learning MPC course is in preparation for summer delivery. Prospective PhD students with a background in control, optimization, or applied mathematics are welcome to get in touch.
